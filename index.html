WAIST CIRCUMFERENCE
Midpoint between inferior border of lowest palpable rib and top of iliac crest (WHO 2008). Measured at end of normal expiration, patient standing.
— Approximately 2–3 cm above the umbilicus in normal-weight adults.
— Do NOT use the umbilicus as the landmark — it migrates downward in obesity, introducing systematic error.
— Tape must be horizontal to the floor all the way around.
HIP CIRCUMFERENCE
Maximum circumference of the buttocks — at the level of the greater trochanters (GT) anteriorly and maximum gluteal protrusion posteriorly (WHO 2008).
— Tape horizontal to floor — check from the side that it is not running obliquely.
— Patient standing, feet together, weight evenly distributed.
— Measurer positioned laterally to confirm horizontal alignment.
For calibration: These WHO 2008 landmarks are what the algorithm correction is trained on. Consistent landmark use across all 50 calibration patients is essential for a valid regression.
