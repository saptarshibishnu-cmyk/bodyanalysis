Why 360° video supersedes 3-photo capture

1

Complete body envelope

A single slow rotation provides frontal (0°), oblique (45°, 135°, 225°, 315°), lateral (90°, 270°), and posterior (180°) views. This gives full 360° silhouette coverage for true 3D circumference reconstruction — versus only 3 views in the previous version, which missed anterior oblique and posterior oblique contours critical for accurate circumference estimation in non-symmetrical body shapes.

2

Automatic angle detection

Shoulder-width tracking via BlazePose landmark pairs (L-shoulder / R-shoulder) provides a real-time rotation proxy. As the patient turns, the shoulder projection width changes sinusoidally — width maximum = facing camera (0°/180°), width minimum = lateral view (90°/270°). The algorithm triggers a frame capture when the tracked angle crosses each 45° target within a ±10° window.

3

Multi-frame averaging

With 8 frames, waist width estimates from the 0° and 180° views are averaged (anterior and posterior frontal), and depth estimates from the 90° and 270° views are averaged. This reduces per-frame noise and asymmetry artefacts. The Ramanujan ellipse formula receives the mean half-axes (a = mean frontal half-width, b = mean lateral half-depth), giving a statistically more robust circumference estimate than any single frame.

4

Single patient interaction

The patient performs one natural movement — turning in a circle — rather than holding three static poses. This is faster, less instructions-dependent, and more feasible in elderly or mobility-limited patients who may struggle with prolonged static positioning.

Circumference estimation from 360° frames

Waist width frontal (W_f) = mean of 0° and 180° frame widths at waist level
Waist depth lateral (W_d) = mean of 90° and 270° frame depths at waist level
Waist circumference = π × (3(a+b) − √((3a+b)(a+3b))) where a=W_f/2, b=W_d/2 (Ramanujan)
Hip width frontal (H_f) = mean of 0° and 180° frame widths at hip level
Hip depth lateral (H_d) = mean of 90° and 270° frame depths at hip level
Hip circumference = π × (3(a+b) − √((3a+b)(a+3b))) where a=H_f/2, b=H_d/2

Oblique frames (45°, 135°, 225°, 315°) provide redundant width estimates used for inter-frame consistency checks and to detect asymmetric abdominal protrusion (e.g. diastasis recti, unilateral lipodystrophy).
